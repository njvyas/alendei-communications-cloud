# Billing & Financial Architecture

Note: GST rate/rule references below are architectural placeholders describing *where* tax logic plugs into the system, not a statement of current tax rates or rules. Verify with a qualified professional before acting on any specific GST/invoicing configuration.

## 1. Core principle

> **Financial truth comes from the immutable usage ledger, never from dashboard aggregates.**

`usage_ledger` (`DATABASE.md` §9) is append-only. Wallet balances, credit-used amounts, invoice totals, and every billing dashboard number are **materialized/derived views recomputed from the ledger**, never hand-maintained counters that could drift from it. If a materialized balance and a fresh ledger aggregation ever disagree, the ledger aggregation is correct by definition, and the discrepancy is a bug to fix, not a number to reconcile away.

## 2. Ledger entry types

| `entry_type` | Meaning | Sign convention |
|---|---|---|
| `provider_cost` | Actual cost incurred from the provider for an attempt | Debit against org's cost basis (internal, not customer-visible by default) |
| `customer_charge` | Amount billed to the tenant for a message/service | Debit against wallet/credit |
| `reseller_markup` | Reseller's margin on a customer charge | Credit to reseller's revenue view |
| `adjustment` | Manual correction (goodwill credit, billing error fix) | Either direction, always references the `adjustments` row justifying it |
| `refund` | Reversal of a prior charge | Credit against wallet/credit |
| `recharge` | Wallet top-up (manual or auto) | Credit to wallet |

Every entry references the originating `message_id`/`message_attempt_id` or `ai_usage_id` where applicable, so any charge is traceable back to the exact event that caused it — required for both customer dispute resolution and audit.

## 3. Prepaid / postpaid / wallet / credit

- **Prepaid**: **available balance** (`wallets.balance - wallets.reserved`, `DATABASE.md` §10) must cover the *estimated* charge before a chargeable send is authorized — never raw `balance` alone (see §8's reservation flow for why "check then act" against raw balance is unsafe under concurrency).
- **Postpaid**: governed by `credit_accounts.credit_limit`; the same reservation discipline applies against `credit_limit - used - reserved`.
- **Auto-recharge**: `wallets.auto_recharge_config` defines a threshold and top-up amount; crossing the threshold emits `alendei.billing.wallet_low_balance.v1` (`EVENTS.md`), consumed by a recharge workflow (payment provider integration is out of scope for Phase 0 — no real payment processor is connected).

## 4. Invoicing & GST

`invoices` are generated by aggregating `usage_ledger` rows for an org over a period; `gst_amount`/`total` are computed at generation time from the org's applicable tax configuration (rate/HSN/SAC mapping is a configuration input, not hard-coded) — again, confirm current applicable rates/rules with a qualified professional before finalizing tax logic. Invoices are immutable once `issued`; a correction after issuance is a new `adjustments` + `usage_ledger` entry plus, where required, a credit note — never an edit to the issued invoice row.

## 5. Fallback-chain billing interaction (formal policy — `organizations.billing_policy`)

Per `FALLBACK_ENGINE.md` §7: every attempt that actually reaches a provider generates its own `provider_cost` ledger entry (real cost is real regardless of outcome). `customer_charge` behavior is governed by the org's `billing_policy` (`DATABASE.md` §2), one of exactly two supported values — this is a resolved, formal policy field, not an undefined future decision:

| `billing_policy` | Behavior | When `customer_charge` entries are written |
|---|---|---|
| `charge_per_logical_message` (platform default) | One charge per logical message | On chain terminal state: the standard rate if any attempt succeeded; zero, or a configurable reduced "failed delivery" fee (org rate-card setting), if the entire chain failed |
| `charge_per_attempt` (opt-in) | One charge per attempt that reached a provider | On each attempt reaching a terminal outcome, regardless of individual success/failure — appropriate for orgs whose contractual pricing passes through raw provider cost per try |

**Worked example** against the platform's canonical five-attempt chain (WhatsApp A fail → WhatsApp B fail → RCS A fail → RCS B fail → SMS A success), under each policy:

- `charge_per_logical_message`: 5 `provider_cost` entries (one per attempt that reached a provider), exactly **1** `customer_charge` entry, recorded when the message reaches `DELIVERED` on attempt 5.
- `charge_per_attempt`: 5 `provider_cost` entries **and** 5 `customer_charge` entries, one per attempt.

## 6. Reseller markup & revenue reporting

`resellers.default_markup_pct` (overridable per organization) determines the `reseller_markup` entries generated alongside `customer_charge` entries for organizations under that reseller. Revenue reporting (`/reports`) aggregates `usage_ledger` filtered by `reseller_id`/`org_id`/date range — never a separately maintained revenue number.

## 7. Refunds & adjustments

Both are new ledger rows, never mutations of history. `adjustments` records the business justification (reason, approver) and links to the `usage_ledger` row(s) it produced, giving a full "why was this charged/credited" trail for support and audit.

## 8. Concurrent-spend safety — reservation, not check-then-act

Naively reading a balance, checking it's sufficient, then sending is unsafe: two concurrent sends for the same org can both read the same (still-sufficient) balance before either one's charge is recorded, overspending the wallet or exceeding the credit limit. ACC instead uses an **atomic reservation** (`DATABASE.md` §10a):

```
Calculate expected charge (worst case across the full configured fallback chain, per §5's policy — a
                            charge_per_attempt org reserves for all configured steps; a
                            charge_per_logical_message org reserves for one charge at the standard rate)
        ↓
SELECT ... FOR UPDATE on the org's wallet/credit_account row  (a real row lock — this is what
                                                                 makes the next step atomic against
                                                                 concurrent sends for the SAME org)
        ↓
Verify available balance (balance - reserved, or credit_limit - used - reserved) ≥ expected charge
        ↓
INSERT usage_ledger row, entry_type='reservation', amount=expected charge   (same transaction)
        ↓
COMMIT  (releases the row lock; wallets.reserved is a materialized sum recomputed to include this
         reservation)
        ↓
Authorize the send (Eligibility Engine consults available balance as computed above, never a stale read)
        ↓
... send proceeds through the routing/fallback pipeline ...
        ↓
On chain terminal state: INSERT usage_ledger row, entry_type='reservation_release', amount = (reserved
   amount - actual customer_charge), and the actual entry_type='customer_charge' row for the real amount
```

This closes the concurrent-overspend race the same way `FALLBACK_ENGINE.md` §4 closes the escalation race: with a real database transaction as the correctness boundary, not an application-level "read then decide" sequence. It correctly handles: concurrent sends (each gets its own row-locked reservation, sequentially, never both reading a stale balance), worker retries (idempotent — a retried send reuses the same `message_id`/`idempotency_key`, so it cannot create a second reservation for the same logical message), duplicate events (the billing-ledger-writer consumer is itself idempotent per `EVENTS.md` §3), fallback attempts (covered by reserving for the worst case up front, per §5's policy), refunds (always a new ledger entry, never a reservation mutation), and provider timeouts (a timed-out attempt that never reaches a terminal state within a bounded overall chain timeout still resolves to a terminal `FAILED`/`EXPIRED` message state, which still triggers the reservation-release step).

**Precise accounting model (do not conflate `reserved` with a deduction from `balance`)**: a `reservation` entry increases `wallets.reserved`/`credit_accounts.reserved` only — it never additionally decreases `wallets.balance`/`credit_accounts.used`. `balance`/`used` change only when an actual financial fact is recorded: `customer_charge`, `refund`, `adjustment`, or `recharge`. `reservation_release` decreases `reserved` back down once the real charge is known. `available_balance = balance - reserved` remains the single quantity the Eligibility Engine checks; the full normative definition, consistent across this document, `DATABASE.md` §10, and `API.md`, lives in `DATABASE.md` §10.

## 9. Formal financial relationship

Ledger entries are never assumed to satisfy `customer_charge = provider_cost` — the two are computed independently and related through an explicit pricing step, both persisted for full reproducibility:

```
provider_cost            (what the provider actually billed ACC for this attempt — from provider_credentials'
                           scope and the provider's own rate, or the simulator's configured cost in dev/test)
        ↓
ACC pricing engine        (applies the organization's rate card — a versioned config, whose resulting
                           Pricing Evaluation is referenced by usage_ledger.pricing_evaluation_id,
                           DATABASE.md §10/§10b — never a hard-coded markup)
        ↓
customer_charge            (per §5's billing_policy)
        ↓
reseller_markup             (the reseller's configured margin on customer_charge, DATABASE.md §2's
                           default_markup_pct, overridable per organization)
        ↓
tax / discount / adjustment  (GST and any discretionary discount/adjustment applied on top, each its
                           own ledger entry_type, §4/§10)
```

Every arrow above is a distinct, independently-inspectable `usage_ledger` entry — never a single computed number with the intermediate steps discarded. This is what lets support/finance answer "why was this org charged X" by reading the ledger rows for one `message_id`, rather than re-deriving the calculation from current (possibly since-changed) rate-card config.

## 10. Transaction-specific pricing architecture — no universal price per message

**Terminology (generalizing billing beyond messaging — do not conflate these four concepts)**:

- **Logical communication / logical message** — a `messages` row (`DATABASE.md` §6): one instance of a caller's intent to communicate over a channel (WhatsApp/SMS/RCS/Email/Voice send). This remains exactly what `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5's message model owns; nothing about the generalization below changes it.
- **Physical delivery attempt** — a `message_attempts` row: one provider/channel try at fulfilling a logical message.
- **Billable transaction** — the unit the Pricing & Rating Engine and the org's billing policy actually operate over. For messaging channels, a billable transaction *is* a logical message (or, under `charge_per_attempt`, an attempt) — the common case, and the one this document mostly illustrates. For non-messaging or hybrid communication types — a Voice call, a Voice AI session, a standalone AI inference call, a metered API usage event, a platform service usage event — the billable transaction is the corresponding **logical communication lifecycle** for that type, which need not be a `messages` row at all. `usage_ledger` already establishes this pattern (`ai_usage_id` alongside `message_id`/`message_attempt_id`, `DATABASE.md` §§10–11); the same shape extends conceptually to future transaction types without requiring the message/attempt model to represent something it structurally isn't.
- **Financial ledger entry** — a `usage_ledger` row: the immutable financial fact produced by rating and billing one billable transaction.

Wherever this document set states "one financially authoritative billing lifecycle/outcome per logical message," the general form of that guarantee is **per billable transaction or logical communication lifecycle** — a logical message is the common case, not the universal one. This generalization changes no existing guarantee; it only widens what the guarantee is stated to apply to, so that Voice/Voice AI/AI/API-usage/platform-service billing are covered by the same architecture rather than requiring a parallel one.

Phase 0.2 strategic correction: the billing architecture must not assume one universal price per message. As ACC's channel and product scope grows (SMS, RCS, WhatsApp, Email, Voice, Voice AI, campaigns, journeys, API usage, reseller traffic, white-label customers, international traffic, negotiated enterprise pricing — `PRD.md` §1), "what happened," "what it's worth," and "what is financially charged" must remain three separately-inspectable concepts, never collapsed into one lookup:

```
Usage / Billable Transaction       (what happened — a message_attempt, an AI call, a voice-minute, a campaign send, §10 above)
        ↓
Pricing & Rating Engine             (what that transaction is worth — §§11–14 below)
        ↓
Pricing Evaluation                   (HOW the amount was calculated — §16 below)
        ↓
Pricing Evaluation Components         (the individual calculation line items making up that evaluation — §16 below)
        ↓
Billing Decision / Financial Treatment  (Billing Outcome: what is financially charged/recorded, per the org's billing_policy — §5 above)
        ↓
Immutable Usage Ledger                 (usage_ledger — §1 above, the sole source of financial truth for WHAT happened)
        ↓
Wallet / Credit / Invoice               (materialized/derived views — §§1, 3–4 above)
```

**The Pricing & Rating Engine, and everything it produces (a Pricing Evaluation and its Components), is conceptually independent of the immutable ledger — and the boundary between them is not merely conceptual, it is a boundary about what kind of fact each one records.** A **Pricing Evaluation** (with its **Pricing Evaluation Components**) explains **HOW** an amount was calculated: which plan/version/rule priced it, the rating basis, the quantity/unit/unit-price/amount of each independent component, and the provider-cost/customer-price split at both the evaluation and component level (§16 below). The **Usage Ledger** is the authoritative financial record of **WHAT** actually, financially happened: a `provider_cost`, `customer_charge`, `reseller_markup`, `tax`, `discount`, `adjustment`, `refund`, `recharge`, `reservation`, or `reservation_release` entry (§2 above) — nothing more granular than that. **The ledger must never become a calculation-detail table**: it does not store per-component breakdowns, unit prices, or rating math — it references the pricing evaluation that produced an amount via the authoritative concrete foreign key `usage_ledger.pricing_evaluation_id` (`DATABASE.md` §10) and records the resulting financial fact. (`rate_card_ref` remains present as historical/descriptive metadata only — a display label, never the relationship an audit joins on.) This is why a pricing config change can never retroactively alter a historical charge (§15 below) — the ledger entry is the immutable fact; the pricing evaluation is the (equally immutable, but separately-purposed) explanation of how that fact's amount was arrived at.

## 11. Pricing & Rating Engine — what it determines

For a given transaction, the Pricing & Rating Engine determines: provider cost, base customer price, reseller markup, discount, tax, final customer charge, currency, pricing plan, pricing version, pricing rule, and rating basis. This generalizes the `provider_cost → pricing engine → customer_charge → reseller_markup → tax/discount/adjustment` chain already established in §9 above — §9's chain is what the engine computes; this section and §§12–17 below are what the engine is built *from*. The engine's output is recorded as a **Pricing Evaluation** (with **Pricing Evaluation Components** where the transaction is composite) — §16 below defines that record precisely, including its explicit boundary against the immutable ledger.

## 12. Pricing dimensions

Pricing may depend on any of: organization, reseller, workspace, channel, transaction type, provider, destination, country/region, message type, template/category, quantity, duration, billable units, campaign/journey, volume tier, contract, negotiated price, or an explicit pricing override. **No pricing rule is required to use every dimension** — a simple flat per-message SMS rate uses almost none of them; a negotiated enterprise Voice AI contract may use most of them. The conceptual schema home for these rules is `pricing_rules` (`DATABASE.md` §10b).

## 13. Pricing models supported

The architecture must support, without requiring a formula to be implemented yet (extensibility, not implementation, is the Phase 0.2 requirement):

- Per message; per unit; per minute; per second; minimum billable duration; rounded duration.
- Per conversation/session; per category; destination-specific; provider-specific.
- Volume/tier pricing; fixed/negotiated pricing; hybrid pricing (a composite of the above).

**Example — Voice AI** (illustrative of why a single "price per message" model cannot hold): a single Voice AI transaction's worth is a composite of `connection fee + voice duration + AI processing + TTS/STT`, each potentially priced on a different basis (flat, per-minute, per-unit) and potentially sourced from a different cost basis (telephony provider, AI model provider). This is exactly why pricing rules are dimensioned and composable (§12), not a single per-transaction lookup.

## 14. Provider cost vs. customer price — explicit separation

`provider_cost` is never assumed equal to `customer_charge`. The full chain, each step its own independently-inspectable, persisted fact (already formalized in §9 above; restated here as the normative summary this section's pricing architecture builds toward):

```
Provider Cost → Base Customer Price → Reseller Markup → Discount → Tax → Final Customer Charge
```

This separation is what makes reseller and white-label commercial models possible at all: a reseller's markup (`resellers.default_markup_pct`, overridable per organization, §6 above) is applied *on top of* the base customer price the Pricing & Rating Engine computed, never baked into `provider_cost` itself, and a reseller can set its own base pricing (`customer_pricing_assignments`, `DATABASE.md` §10b) independent of Alendei's own platform-default pricing plan.

## 15. Pricing versioning — historical transactions are never re-rated

Pricing is versioned exactly the way routing policy is versioned (`ROUTING_ENGINE.md` §5): every pricing plan has versions, exactly one is effective at a time per scope, and a historical transaction's `usage_ledger.pricing_evaluation_id` (`DATABASE.md` §10, §10b) — the authoritative relational reference, not the descriptive `rate_card_ref` — points at the specific `pricing_evaluations` row (which in turn names the `pricing_plan_version`/`pricing_rule`(s) actually used) that rated it. **ACC never recalculates a historical transaction using current pricing configuration** — a pricing plan edit takes effect only for transactions rated after the edit, exactly as a routing policy edit takes effect only for attempts routed after the edit.

For any past transaction, the system must be able to answer, without ambiguity and without re-deriving from current config: which pricing plan, which pricing version, which rule, the provider cost, the base price, the markup, the discount, the tax, the final charge, the rating basis, and the currency. This is the same "explain a historical charge from the ledger, not from re-running current logic" principle already stated for billing generally in §9's closing paragraph — pricing versioning is what makes that principle hold even after pricing configuration changes.

## 16. Attempt-level pricing — Pricing Evaluation & Pricing Components

`messages` remains the logical message; `message_attempts` remains the record of every physical attempt; `usage_ledger` remains financial truth (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §5, `DATABASE.md` §6) — pricing does not disturb this ownership boundary. Each `message_attempt` (or, for a non-messaging billable transaction, `BILLING.md` §10, whatever row represents it) can retain its own applicable provider, routing policy/version (`routing_policy_id`/`routing_policy_version_id`), and a single reference to a **pricing evaluation** (`pricing_evaluation_id`) — the record of how that transaction was rated.

**Why a single `pricing_rule_id` is not enough.** A simple SMS or WhatsApp send is fully explained by one rule at one rate. A Voice AI transaction is not: its total is a composite of independently-priced, independently-costed parts (connection/telephony, speech-to-text, LLM/AI processing, text-to-speech), each potentially on a different rating basis and a different provider cost basis (§13). A single `pricing_rule_id` column cannot represent "this total is the sum of these four independently-rated parts." A **Pricing Evaluation** can:

```
Billable Transaction / Attempt
            ↓
      Pricing Evaluation        (pricing_plan, pricing_plan_version, rating_timestamp, currency,
            ↓                    rating_basis, total_provider_cost, total_customer_price)
   ┌────────┼────────┐
   ↓        ↓        ↓
Component  Component  Component   (component_type, pricing_rule reference, quantity, unit,
   ↓        ↓        ↓             unit_price, amount, provider_cost component, customer_price component)
Telephony   STT      LLM/TTS
```

A **pricing evaluation** (`pricing_evaluations`, `DATABASE.md` §10b) records: pricing plan, pricing plan version, rating timestamp, currency, rating basis, total provider cost, and total customer price — the single row an attempt references (`pricing_evaluation_id`) to explain how it was rated, regardless of whether that rating involved one component or several.

A **pricing component** (`pricing_evaluation_components`, `DATABASE.md` §10b) records, per independent part of the rating: component type, the pricing rule/reference that priced it, quantity, unit, unit price, amount, and — where applicable — its own provider-cost component and customer-price component, keeping provider cost and customer price separate at the component level exactly as they are kept separate at the transaction level (§14).

**Worked example — Voice AI transaction** (illustrative; not a commitment to these exact rates):

```
Telephony:      2.5 minutes × ₹0.80/min       = ₹2.00
STT:            2.5 minutes × ₹0.12/min       = ₹0.30
LLM:            8,000 tokens × ₹0.05/1K tokens = ₹0.40
TTS:            2.5 minutes × ₹0.10/min       = ₹0.25
Platform fee:   flat                          = ₹1.00
                                                 ------
Total customer price:                           ₹3.95
```

Each line is its own `pricing_evaluation_components` row (its own `component_type`, `quantity`/`unit`/`unit_price`/`amount`, and its own provider-cost/customer-price split where a provider cost applies — e.g. telephony/STT/TTS may each have a distinct provider cost basis, while the platform fee may have none); the `pricing_evaluations` row sums them into `total_customer_price = ₹3.95` (and, symmetrically, a `total_provider_cost`) and is what the attempt/transaction actually references. The architecture can therefore explain the total exactly, component by component, not just assert it.

Example — attempt-level referencing across a fallback chain, `WhatsApp → RCS → SMS` (the ordinary, single-component case — most channel sends need only one component per evaluation):

```
Attempt 1 → WhatsApp   (pricing_evaluation E1: 1 component, provider cost C1)
Attempt 2 → RCS        (pricing_evaluation E2: 1 component, provider cost C2)
Attempt 3 → SMS        (pricing_evaluation E3: 1 component, provider cost C3)
```

**No attempt duplicates an entire pricing configuration or copies every component onto itself** — `message_attempts.pricing_evaluation_id` is a single foreign key; the components live once, on `pricing_evaluation_components`, referenced (never copied) via the evaluation (`DATABASE.md` §6, §10b).

**The boundary this section exists to keep sharp**: a Pricing Evaluation (and its Components) explains **HOW** an amount was calculated — plan, version, rule, rating basis, currency, quantity/unit/unit-price, component amounts, provider-cost/customer-price components, and negotiated-pricing/other calculation context (`DATABASE.md` §10b). The Usage Ledger records **WHAT** financially happened — one of the fixed `entry_type` values in §2 above, and nothing more granular. A ledger row references the evaluation that priced it via the concrete foreign key `usage_ledger.pricing_evaluation_id` (`DATABASE.md` §10) — a direct, walkable relationship, not an ambiguous or polymorphic one — never inlining the evaluation's component breakdown; `rate_card_ref` remains only as historical/descriptive metadata alongside it, never the reference an audit or reconciliation process resolves against. The evaluation is never itself treated as a financial fact. Neither table is ever allowed to absorb the other's job.

## 17. Billing Policy vs. Pricing Policy — explicitly independent

These two concepts govern different questions and must never be conflated:

| Concept | Answers | Examples | Defined in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Billing Policy** | HOW / WHEN the customer is charged | `charge_per_logical_message`, `charge_per_attempt`, prepaid, postpaid, reservation rules | `organizations.billing_policy` (`DATABASE.md` §2), §5 above |
| **Pricing Policy / Pricing Plan** | HOW MUCH the transaction is worth | SMS ₹X, WhatsApp category-specific pricing, destination-based pricing, Voice ₹X/minute, negotiated enterprise pricing | `pricing_plans`/`pricing_plan_versions`/`pricing_rules` (`DATABASE.md` §10b), §§10–16 above |

An org's billing policy determines *when and how many* `customer_charge` ledger entries a fallback chain produces (§5's worked example); its pricing plan determines *what rate* each of those entries is computed at. The two vary independently — an org can be `charge_per_attempt` under a flat per-message pricing plan, or `charge_per_logical_message` under a destination-tiered plan — and neither document, table, or module is ever permitted to assume the other from its own value.
